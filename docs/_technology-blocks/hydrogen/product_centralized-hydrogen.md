Technologies

| Technology | What it is | Type |
|:--------------------------|:----------------------------------------------------------|:----------------|
| Steam methane reformer | Reforms natural gas with steam, then shifts to hydrogen and CO₂. | Conventional |
| Steam methane reformer with capture on the shift gas | A steam methane reformer whose shifted syngas, where the CO₂ is already at high partial pressure, is sent to capture. | Alternative |
| Water electrolysis, low temperature, central plant | Centralized alkaline or PEM electrolysis plant, about 100 MW to 1 GW, supplying a hydrogen pipeline or merchant market. | Alternative |
| Autothermal reformer (hydrogen) | Partial oxidation with oxygen in one vessel; suits high capture rates. | Prospective |
| Autothermal reforming to hydrogen with full capture | An oxygen-blown autothermal reformer designed to send nearly all its CO₂, in one concentrated stream, to capture. | Prospective |
| Biomass or waste gasification to hydrogen | Gasifies biomass or waste to syngas for fuels or hydrogen. | Prospective |
| Coal or petroleum-coke gasification to hydrogen with capture | A gasifier with a shift reactor that sends its CO₂ in one concentrated stream to capture; biomass or waste feed makes it a removal. | Prospective |
| Methane pyrolysis | Splits methane into hydrogen and solid carbon, with no CO₂ to capture. | Prospective |
| Solid-oxide electrolysis | High-temperature steam electrolysis with higher electrical efficiency. | Prospective |
| Steam methane reformer with capture on the flue gas | A steam methane reformer whose reformer-furnace flue gas (dilute CO₂) is sent to post-combustion capture. The capture unit itself is a separate technology. | Prospective |
